30 Years, Millions of Surgeries: What the Data Says

LASIK received US FDA approval in the 1990s and has since become the world's most performed vision-correction surgery. Across large published studies and registries, patient satisfaction consistently reports in the mid-to-high 90s percent — among the highest of any elective procedure in modern medicine.

Sight-threatening complications are rare (well below 1% in screened populations), and the most common issues — temporary dryness, night glare — are usually transient and manageable with proper post-operative care. Modern blade-free flaps, eye-trackers that follow micro-movements hundreds of times per second, and topography-guided planning have pushed safety further with each generation of technology.

To put these numbers in context: peer-reviewed analyses have shown that decades of daily contact lens wear carries a cumulative risk of serious eye infection that compares unfavourably with one-time modern LASIK for suitable candidates. Yet most patients never hesitate before putting a lens on their eye every morning — a comparison worth making honestly at any LASIK consultation.

Real Side Effects — The Honest List

Every medical procedure carries risks, and honest surgeons discuss all of them before you sign anything. Here is the complete, unfiltered list of what can happen after LASIK — and the honest context for each:

  • Dry eyes: The most common effect, expected for weeks to a few months post-procedure, managed effectively with lubricating drops. The LASIK flap creation temporarily reduces corneal sensation, which reduces the reflex to blink and produce tears. Pre-existing severe dryness is screened out before surgery — which is precisely why pre-op evaluation matters.
  • Night glare and halos: Common in the early healing weeks, settling as the eye adapts to its new shape. Topography-guided profiles (Contoura Vision) reduce this further by smoothing irregular micro-aberrations. Most patients notice significant improvement by week 6–8.
  • Under- or over-correction: A small percentage of patients land slightly off their target power after stabilisation. An enhancement (touch-up) procedure addresses this after the eye stabilises — typically beyond 12 months. Ask any clinic, including ours, for their written enhancement policy before surgery.
  • Flap-related issues: Rare with modern femtosecond flaps and proper aftercare. The "no rubbing" instruction in the weeks after surgery exists because the flap, while secure, needs time to fully re-adhere. This is aftercare discipline, not fragility.
  • Regression: Minor power return over years in some patients, more common in high prescriptions. The 12-month stability rule for eligibility exists precisely to minimise this — if your power is still changing, the right advice is to wait.
Important context: The side effects listed above are the same ones patients experience with contact lenses — dryness, glare, blur — often chronically, without the prospect of resolution. LASIK's versions are typically temporary in screened candidates.

5 Myths vs Facts — The Ones That Won't Die

Misinformation about LASIK is remarkably persistent — the same myths circulate every decade, slightly updated. Here they are, addressed directly:

❌

Myth 1: "LASIK causes blindness"

Fact: Sight-threatening outcomes are extraordinarily rare in properly screened patients. Statistically, long-term daily contact lens wear carries its own meaningful infection and complication risks that patients rarely compare against LASIK. The cases of serious complications that do exist almost universally involve skipped screening, ignored contraindications, or post-op aftercare non-compliance.

❌

Myth 2: "Effects wear off after 10 years"

Fact: The corneal reshaping performed by LASIK is permanent — the tissue removed does not grow back. What changes at age 40+ is presbyopia (age-related difficulty reading), which affects everyone regardless of whether they had LASIK. Confusing age-related vision change with LASIK "wearing off" is one of the most common misunderstandings we address.

❌

Myth 3: "You can't have cataract surgery after LASIK"

Fact: You absolutely can — and many patients do. The cataract surgeon simply uses your pre-LASIK corneal records for lens power calculations. This is why we provide every LASIK patient a permanent record of their pre-op corneal measurements. Keep your surgical reports safely — they matter decades later.

❌

Myth 4: "Winter or summer matters for surgery"

Fact: Season is clinically irrelevant to LASIK outcomes. What matters is screening quality, surgical precision, and aftercare discipline — none of which varies with weather. The "best season" advice is marketing, not medicine.

❌

Myth 5: "If my eye moves, the laser will cut wrong"

Fact: Modern laser systems track eye movements at hundreds of times per second and pause within milliseconds if your eye moves beyond tolerance — faster than any human reflex. This technology has been standard for over 15 years. The days of needing perfect stillness belong to a previous generation of equipment.

❌

Myth 6: "Cheap LASIK is just as good"

Fact: LASIK is one procedure where the equipment genuinely matters. Older laser platforms don't retire — they get discounted. The topography, eye tracker, laser platform and flap method should be named in writing in your quote. "LASIK" covers a wide range of technology; the price gap usually reflects that range honestly.

How Technology Reduced Risk — A Short History

Early-2000s LASIK used mechanical microkeratome blades to create the corneal flap, and the laser corrected only the spectacle prescription numbers — no personalisation. Complications from blade flaps, while uncommon, were the origin of many of the horror stories that still circulate today.

Modern LASIK at a properly equipped centre uses:

  1. Femtosecond laser for flap creation — entirely blade-free, with micron-level precision. The flap is more predictable, more uniform, and complications from flap creation are now extremely rare.
  2. Corneal topography and wavefront mapping — thousands of measurement points capture the unique optical fingerprint of your cornea, not just the prescription numbers.
  3. Real-time eye-tracking — the laser system monitors your eye's position hundreds of times per second and compensates or pauses instantly.
  4. Topography-guided ablation (Contoura) — the laser corrects both the prescription AND the cornea's individual micro-irregularities, improving optical quality beyond what glasses achieve. Learn about Contoura Vision →

Most of the complications people still fear belong to a previous technological era. One reason "cheapest LASIK" deals deserve scrutiny: old machines don't retire — they get discounted. Technology should be named, not implied.

What the Post-Op Period Looks Like

Understanding what to expect after surgery removes the anxiety that comes from surprise. Here is an honest account of the recovery experience for a straightforward LASIK case:

📅

Day of Surgery

The procedure itself takes approximately 10–15 minutes per eye. Immediately after, vision is blurry — expected. Eyes are light-sensitive and teary. Rest is required; most patients sleep through the first several hours. Vision begins clearing within hours.

🌅

Day 1–3

Significant improvement is usually visible by the next-morning review. Mild scratchiness and dryness are normal. Drops are critical — lubricating drops as needed, antibiotic and anti-inflammatory drops on schedule. No rubbing under any circumstances.

🗓️

Week 1–2

Most desk-work patients return to screens within 2–3 days. Vision continues sharpening. Night glare and halos are present but typically manageable. Outdoor dark glasses protect from dust and glare.

✅

Month 1–3

Vision stabilises progressively. Final assessment of correction adequacy happens after stabilisation — typically 3–6 months. Enhancement (if needed) is evaluated after the one-year mark for most cases.

Frequently Asked Questions

Kya LASIK 100% safe hai?

Medicine mein kuch bhi 100% nahi hota — lekin sahi screening ke baad LASIK duniya ki sabse safe elective surgeries mein hai. Serious complications 1% se bahut kam cases mein hote hain, aur zyadatar side effects (dryness, glare) temporary hote hain. Screening ki quality hi asli safety factor hai.

What is the biggest risk of LASIK?

Statistically: getting it done on an unsuitable eye. That's why the pre-surgery scan matters more than the surgery itself. Among properly screened patients, the common issues are temporary dryness and night glare, which typically resolve within weeks to months. The cases of serious outcomes almost universally involve skipped or inadequate screening.

Has anyone gone blind from LASIK?

Sight-threatening complications are extraordinarily rare and usually linked to skipped screening, untreated infection, or trauma to a fresh flap. Following aftercare instructions — particularly no rubbing and no water in the eye for the instructed period — keeps the already-small risk smaller. In properly screened, properly managed patients, permanent vision loss is vanishingly rare.

Is LASIK safer than wearing contact lenses for life?

Peer-reviewed analyses suggest decades of contact lens wear carries cumulative infection risk (particularly with extended or overnight wear) that compares unfavourably with one-time modern LASIK for suitable candidates. This isn't an argument to rush into surgery — it's an argument to compare risk honestly, not assume contacts are always the "safe" choice.

What if my LASIK result isn't perfect?

A small percentage of patients need an enhancement after stabilisation — more common in higher prescriptions. Ask any clinic, including us, for their written enhancement policy before surgery. A clinic that won't give you this in writing is not a clinic you should trust with your eyes.

Kya LASIK ke baad chasma phir se lag sakta hai?

LASIK ka result permanent hai — cornea ki jo reshaping hui hai woh vapas nahi aati. Lekin age ke saath 40+ ke baad presbyopia (padhne ki takleef) hoti hai jo LASIK se independent hai — yeh sab logo ko hoti hai. High power cases mein thodi regression possible hai, isliye 12-month stability rule exist karta hai.
