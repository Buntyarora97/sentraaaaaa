Monofocal vs Multifocal vs Toric — Is Premium Worth It?

Think of it as one-time pricing for decades of daily use. A monofocal lens gives excellent distance vision — with reading glasses. A trifocal spreads clarity across distance, computer and reading — most patients rarely reach for glasses again. If you have astigmatism, a toric version protects the sharpness you're paying for. Worth it? For a 60-year-old who reads, uses a phone and drives — very often, yes. Dr. Modi will tell you honestly if your eye won't benefit from a premium lens — not every eye is suited for multifocals, and we say so.

Monofocal

Best for value-focused patients. Delivers sharp distance vision — reading glasses will still be needed for near tasks. The most widely covered lens under insurance policies.

🌀

Toric

Designed for eyes with astigmatism (cylinder power). Corrects the cylindrical error at the same time as the cataract — protecting the clarity you're investing in.

✨

Multifocal / Trifocal / EDOF

Premium lenses that spread focus across distance, intermediate and near ranges. Most patients are largely glasses-free. EDOF offers a smooth continuous range with fewer halos — ideal for night drivers and screen users.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is cataract surgery free under any government scheme?

Government and charitable programmes offer subsidised basic cataract surgery at empanelled centres. Private packages differ in technology, lens options and experience — we'll help you understand what fits your needs and budget.

Which lens is best for cataract surgery?

There's no universal best — monofocals suit value-focused patients comfortable with reading glasses; trifocals suit those wanting glasses-free range; torics protect astigmatic eyes. Your eye scan plus your lifestyle decides.

Does insurance cover multifocal lenses?

Most policies cover cataract surgery up to a limit; premium lens costs beyond that limit are borne by the patient as a top-up. We verify your exact coverage in writing before surgery.
