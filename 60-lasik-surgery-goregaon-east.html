LASIK vs. Contoura Vision vs. SMILE — What's the Difference?

Goregaon East patients frequently ask about the different types of laser vision correction. Here is a clear, honest comparison:

  • Standard LASIK: Uses a femtosecond laser to create a corneal flap, then an excimer laser to reshape the underlying cornea. Corrects myopia, hyperopia, and astigmatism. Fast recovery. Very effective — but uses an average correction, not customised to your cornea's unique map.
  • Contoura Vision (Topography-Guided LASIK): Same flap-based method as LASIK but uses a detailed corneal topography map (22,000 elevation data points) to guide the laser. This corrects not just your refractive error but also the higher-order aberrations unique to your cornea — the micro-irregularities that cause halos, glare, and night-vision problems. Most patients who previously had starbursts around lights at night see significant improvement. This is our most recommended procedure for Goregaon East patients who spend long hours at screens and driving.
  • SMILE (Small Incision Lenticule Extraction): A newer flapless procedure — the laser creates and removes a small disc of corneal tissue through a 4mm incision, without cutting a flap. No flap means slightly lower dry eye risk post-operatively. Preferred for patients with active lifestyles (contact sports, martial arts). Currently treats only myopia and myopic astigmatism.
  • ICL (Implantable Collamer Lens): Not laser-based. A lens is implanted inside the eye. Best for patients not suitable for LASIK — very high myopia, thin corneas, or dry eye conditions that contraindicate laser surgery.
💡 Which is right for you? The answer depends on your corneal thickness, topography, prescription magnitude, and lifestyle. Dr. Shraddha Surekha will give you an honest, unbiased recommendation during the pre-LASIK workup.

Are You a LASIK Candidate? Eligibility Criteria

Not everyone is a candidate for LASIK — and a surgeon who tells you that you are without proper testing is not one you should trust. The pre-LASIK workup at Sentra Clinic checks all the following:

📅

Age & Stable Prescription

You must be at least 18 years old and your spectacle power should have been stable for at least 1–2 years (no significant change in your last two prescriptions). Performing LASIK on a changing prescription increases the risk of regression. Most Goregaon East patients in their 20s and 30s are in this stable window.

🔬

Corneal Thickness

LASIK removes a thin layer of corneal tissue. Your cornea must be thick enough to allow safe correction. We measure corneal thickness with pachymetry — typically a minimum of 480–500 microns is required pre-operatively. If your corneas are too thin, SMILE or ICL is recommended instead.

🗺️

Corneal Topography (Shape)

The shape of your cornea must be regular. Corneal topography screening detects keratoconus (a progressive corneal thinning condition) — which is an absolute contraindication to LASIK. Performing LASIK on an undetected keratoconus patient can lead to serious vision complications. This test is performed on every patient — no exceptions.

💧

Dry Eye Assessment

Dry eye disease can worsen after LASIK, as the flap creation temporarily disrupts corneal nerves. We evaluate your tear film stability and basal tear secretion before recommending any procedure. Patients with moderate-to-severe dry eye may be recommended SMILE (lower dry eye risk) or ICL instead of standard LASIK.

📊

Prescription Range

LASIK safely corrects: Myopia up to −10 diopters, Hyperopia up to +4 diopters, Astigmatism up to 5 diopters. Beyond these ranges, ICL surgery is safer and gives better visual quality. Your exact candidacy depends on the combination of prescription and corneal thickness available.

❤️

General Health

Certain systemic conditions (uncontrolled autoimmune disease, pregnancy, active keloid formation) are relative contraindications to LASIK. Most healthy Goregaon East adults between 18–45 with stable myopia are excellent candidates. The pre-LASIK workup gives you a definitive answer.

The Pre-LASIK Workup — What Happens on Day 1

One of the most common questions from Goregaon East patients is: How many visits does LASIK require? The answer: the complete evaluation is done on the first visit. Here is exactly what the workup involves:

  1. Remove Contact Lenses Before Visiting — If you wear soft lenses, remove them 3 days before your evaluation. If you wear hard or rigid lenses, stop 2–4 weeks before. Lenses temporarily change your corneal shape — accurate measurements require the cornea to be in its natural state.
  2. Visual Acuity & Auto-Refraction — Measures your current glasses/contact lens prescription with objective technology, plus subjective refinement by the doctor.
  3. Corneal Topography & Aberrometry — A detailed 3D map of your corneal surface. For Contoura Vision, this map actually guides the laser during surgery. Aberrometry measures optical irregularities that glasses cannot correct.
  4. Pachymetry (Corneal Thickness) — Ultrasonic or optical measurement of corneal thickness at multiple points.
  5. Pupil Size Measurement — Your scotopic (dim-light) pupil size is measured. Large pupils in dim light can increase glare/halo symptoms post-LASIK if the ablation zone is not sized appropriately.
  6. Tear Film & Dry Eye Assessment — Schirmer's test or TBUT (tear break-up time) to determine your baseline tear film health.
  7. Dilated Examination by Dr. Shraddha Surekha — The surgeon reviews all test results, examines your corneas under a slit lamp, and assesses the retina. She then discusses her honest recommendation — whether you're a candidate for LASIK, Contoura, SMILE, or ICL — and explains costs, risks, and expected outcomes.

I work long hours at screens at NESCO — will LASIK make my dry eyes worse?

Dry eye is the most common post-LASIK complaint. The risk is assessed and managed pre-operatively. If you have significant dry eye, we may recommend SMILE (lower dry eye risk) or ICL surgery, or pre-treat your dry eye before scheduling LASIK. Post-LASIK, intensive lubricant drops are prescribed for 3–6 months and most patients' dry eye resolves by 3 months.

What if my power is very high — can I still get LASIK?

If your myopia is above approximately −8 to −10 diopters, or if corneal thickness is insufficient after accounting for the required correction, LASIK may not be safe. In those cases, ICL surgery (Implantable Collamer Lens) is the gold standard — giving excellent visual quality without touching the cornea. Sentra Clinic offers ICL as well.

How soon can I play sports / exercise after LASIK?

Non-contact sports (running, gym) can typically resume after 1 week post-LASIK (avoid sweat in eyes). Swimming after 2–4 weeks with goggles. Contact sports (cricket, football, martial arts) — for these, we often recommend SMILE surgery instead, as there is no flap to displace. Discuss your sports activities with Dr. Shraddha during consultation for personalised advice.

Does LASIK hurt?

No. Topical anaesthetic eye drops are used — you feel pressure during the brief suction phase but no pain during the laser treatment. Most patients describe the procedure as surprisingly easy. A mild gritty sensation for a few hours post-procedure is normal and resolves with lubricant drops.
