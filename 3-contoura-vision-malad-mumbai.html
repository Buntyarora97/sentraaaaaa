What Is Contoura Vision and How It Differs from Standard LASIK

Contoura Vision is a topography-guided laser refractive procedure designed to correct not just your spectacle power (myopia, astigmatism, hyperopia) but also the subtle micro-irregularities that exist on every human cornea — irregularities that standard glasses and even conventional LASIK cannot address. It is an evolution of LASIK eye surgery, not a replacement for it, but a meaningfully more personalised version for patients who want the most precise visual outcome possible.

Standard LASIK uses your spectacle prescription — your sphere, cylinder, and axis — as its blueprint. The laser treats the eye based on these numbers alone. Contoura Vision, by contrast, uses a corneal topographer to scan tens of thousands of individual data points on the front surface of your eye before a single laser pulse is fired. This data reveals tiny undulations, asymmetries, and higher-order optical aberrations that your prescription cannot capture. The surgeon then inputs this topographic map into the treatment plan, so the laser corrects your cornea’s actual shape, not just its average curvature.

Think of the difference this way: standard LASIK is like printing a garment from your measurements — it fits well. Contoura Vision is like a bespoke tailor who studies every contour of your body before cutting a single stitch. The result often feels qualitatively different — richer, more natural, sharper — especially in low-light conditions where your pupils dilate and those microscopic surface irregularities become most noticeable.

The Science — Mapping 22,000 Points on Your Cornea

Your cornea is the transparent dome at the front of your eye, and it does roughly 70% of the focusing work your eye performs. Even a perfectly healthy cornea is not a smooth, mathematically perfect sphere. It has subtle high points and low points — micro-elevations measured in thousandths of a millimetre — that differ from person to person and even between your own left and right eyes.

These irregularities scatter incoming light, creating what opticians call higher-order aberrations (HOAs): the ghosting around headlights at night, the starbursts around streetlamps, the sense that your glasses give you 6/6 vision on an eye chart but something still feels “off” in real life. Standard spectacles cannot fix HOAs. Standard LASIK, which treats only the sphere and cylinder, largely ignores them too.

How the Topography Scan Works

Before your Contoura Vision procedure, a corneal topographer — a non-contact, painless device that projects rings of light onto your eye — photographs thousands of reflection points from the corneal surface. Software analyses these reflections to build a three-dimensional elevation map of extraordinary precision. This map identifies not just your average curvature but every peak and valley across the optical zone.

How the Map Becomes Your Treatment

The topographic data is exported and merged with your refraction data inside the laser system’s planning software. The laser’s final treatment profile is therefore a composite: correcting your spectacle power and normalising your corneal surface irregularities simultaneously. The laser fires in a pattern customised specifically to you — no other patient in the world has the same treatment file.

The practical benefit is that after Contoura Vision, many patients report that images feel “cleaner” and driving at night feels noticeably less stressful compared to their pre-surgery experience wearing glasses. This is the direct result of reducing those scattered higher-order aberrations at the source.

A note on expectations: Contoura Vision offers a genuinely higher ceiling for optical quality, but individual results depend on corneal health, power range, and healing. Your surgeon will walk you through realistic expectations after reviewing your topography scan — never before.

The Contoura Vision Procedure — Step-by-Step

The entire Contoura Vision experience at Sentra Clinic is designed to be as comfortable and anxiety-free as possible. Here is exactly what to expect, from your first phone call to your final follow-up visit.

  1. Initial Consultation & Eligibility Scan
    You visit Sentra Clinic in Malad East for a comprehensive pre-operative assessment. This includes corneal topography (the 22,000-point mapping scan), pachymetry to measure corneal thickness, a detailed refraction, intraocular pressure check, and a slit-lamp examination of the anterior and posterior segment. The entire session typically takes 60–90 minutes. Our team reviews the data and tells you honestly whether Contoura Vision, standard LASIK, ICL, or another option is best for your eyes.
  2. Treatment Planning
    If you are a suitable candidate and wish to proceed, the topography data and your refraction are combined inside the laser platform’s planning software. Dr. Surekha, our Cornea & Refractive Specialist, reviews and approves the custom ablation profile. This is the step that makes Contoura Vision genuinely unique — the treatment plan literally does not exist until it is built from your cornea’s map.
  3. Pre-Operative Preparation (Day of Surgery)
    On the day of your procedure, avoid wearing eye make-up, perfume, or scented products (which can interfere with the laser environment). Anaesthetic eye drops are instilled — there are no injections. Within minutes, your eyes are completely numb. The procedure itself is blade-free.
  4. Femtosecond Flap Creation
    A femtosecond laser creates a thin, precise corneal flap in about 20 seconds per eye. You may feel mild pressure, but no pain. The flap is gently folded back to expose the corneal stroma beneath.
  5. Topography-Guided Excimer Laser Reshaping
    The excimer laser fires according to your personalised Contoura treatment profile — correcting your spectacle power and simultaneously smoothing the corneal surface irregularities identified by your topography map. The active laser time is typically 20–60 seconds per eye depending on your prescription.
  6. Flap Repositioning
    The surgeon carefully repositions the corneal flap, which adheres naturally within minutes. No stitches are needed. You rest in our recovery area for a short period while the team confirms the flap is sitting correctly.
  7. Immediate Post-Procedure
    You leave the clinic with protective eyewear. Vision is often usable within hours, though some blurring and mild watering are normal. Antibiotic and anti-inflammatory drops are prescribed. You will be advised not to rub your eyes for several weeks.
  8. Follow-Up Visits
    You return the next day for your first check, then at one week, one month, and three months post-procedure. These visits confirm your refraction, flap integrity, and corneal healing — and give you the opportunity to discuss any questions about your recovery.

Who Should Avoid Contoura Vision

Sentra Clinic’s team believes strongly in honest, patient-first guidance. Contoura Vision is not appropriate for everyone who wants it, and we will always tell you so clearly rather than proceeding with a surgery that is not in your best interest.

You may not be a suitable candidate for Contoura Vision if:

  • Your corneal thickness is insufficient — Contoura removes more tissue than standard LASIK because it corrects surface irregularities in addition to your prescription. A safe residual stromal bed must remain after ablation.
  • You have keratoconus or pellucid marginal degeneration — progressive corneal thinning disorders are a contraindication to any ablative laser procedure. ICL or corneal collagen cross-linking (CXL) may be more appropriate.
  • Your spectacle power is unstable — if your prescription has been changing over the past year, the procedure should be deferred until it has stabilised for at least 12 months.
  • You have significant dry eye syndrome — ablative procedures can worsen dry eye. Moderate-to-severe pre-existing dry eye is better managed with ICL surgery or conservative management first.
  • You are pregnant or breastfeeding — hormonal changes affect corneal curvature and refraction; elective laser surgery is deferred until after this period.
  • Your power exceeds the safe treatment range — very high myopia (beyond approximately −8D) combined with corneal thickness limitations may make Contoura unsuitable; ICL surgery handles high powers without any corneal tissue removal.
  • You have active eye infections or uncontrolled systemic disease — including poorly controlled diabetes with retinal involvement, autoimmune conditions affecting wound healing, or active uveitis.
If your scan shows you are not a Contoura candidate, our team will explain exactly why and present the most appropriate alternative — whether that is standard LASIK, ICL, PRK, or a non-surgical management plan. We never pressure anyone into a procedure they are not suited for.

Frequently Asked Questions About Contoura Vision Surgery

Is Contoura Vision better than standard LASIK?

For suitable candidates, Contoura Vision offers a more personalised and precise correction than standard LASIK because it addresses not just your spectacle power but also the corneal surface irregularities that contribute to night glare, halos, and imperfect visual quality. Whether it is “better” for you specifically depends entirely on what your corneal topography shows. Some patients with perfectly smooth corneas gain little extra from the topographic guidance; others with measurable irregularities gain significantly. Your scan tells us the answer — which is why we always scan before recommending.

How is Contoura Vision different from SMILE or PRK?

All three — Contoura Vision, SMILE, and PRK — are forms of laser vision correction, but they differ in approach. Contoura Vision and PRK use an excimer laser guided by a topographic map; Contoura creates a corneal flap first (like LASIK), while PRK treats the surface directly without a flap. SMILE is a flapless procedure using a femtosecond laser to remove a lenticule of tissue without an excimer laser, and it does not currently offer topography-guided correction. The best choice depends on your corneal profile, power, and lifestyle — our team will guide you after your scan.

Is Contoura Vision surgery painful?

No. Anaesthetic eye drops numb the eye completely before the procedure begins. You may feel mild pressure during the flap creation step, but not pain. After the anaesthetic wears off — typically a few hours later — some patients experience a gritty or watery feeling in the eyes, which usually resolves by the next morning with the prescribed drops. The procedure itself takes about 15 minutes for both eyes.

What is the age limit for Contoura Vision?

Patients must be at least 18 years old, and ideally 21 or older when the prescription tends to be more stable. There is no strict upper age limit, but patients above 40 may be developing presbyopia (reading vision loss with age), which laser surgery does not correct. In such cases, our team discusses monovision options or alternative refractive procedures. The key requirement at any age is prescription stability for at least one year.

Will I need glasses after Contoura Vision?

The goal of Contoura Vision is to achieve spectacle independence for distance vision. The vast majority of suitable candidates achieve 6/6 or better uncorrected vision. However, no surgeon can guarantee this for every patient, and individual healing varies. Additionally, presbyopia (age-related reading difficulty) typically begins around age 40–45 regardless of having had laser surgery, so reading glasses may eventually be needed. Your surgeon will discuss realistic expectations based on your specific prescription and corneal profile.

Is Contoura Vision safe?

Contoura Vision is performed using a well-established femtosecond and excimer laser platform with a strong global safety track record. The addition of topographic guidance is an enhancement to precision, not a new source of risk. As with any surgical procedure, there are possible side effects — including temporary dry eye, mild halos during healing, or, rarely, under- or over-correction. Rigorous pre-operative screening, experienced surgeons, and structured follow-up care are what make the difference between a safe outcome and a preventable complication. At Sentra Clinic, all three are in place.

Can everyone get Contoura Vision?

No — and this is an important point. Contoura Vision has specific eligibility criteria relating to corneal thickness, curvature, and regularity, as well as power range and prescription stability. If your topography shows insufficient corneal tissue, a pattern suggestive of keratoconus, significant dry eye, or another contraindication, we will not recommend Contoura Vision. We will instead recommend the safer, more appropriate option for your eyes — whether that is standard LASIK, ICL surgery, or a non-surgical approach.

Can I get Contoura Vision if I have astigmatism?

Yes, and in fact Contoura Vision is often especially beneficial for patients with astigmatism. Astigmatism arises from corneal irregularity — the very thing Contoura’s topographic mapping is designed to assess and correct precisely. Patients with moderate astigmatism who have tried glasses or toric contact lenses and still experience visual frustration are frequently among those who gain the most from Contoura Vision’s personalised approach. Eligibility still depends on corneal thickness and power limits.
