Retinal Diagnostic Technology at Sentra Clinic

📸

OCT (Optical Coherence Tomography)

High-resolution cross-sectional imaging of the retinal layers — detects fluid, membranes, atrophy, and structural changes at micron precision. Indispensable for diabetic macular oedema, AMD staging, and macular hole assessment. Images are archived for longitudinal comparison at every visit.

📷

Digital Fundus Photography

Wide-field retinal photos documenting the vasculature, optic disc, and peripheral retina. Provides objective baseline and enables comparison over time. Dr. Modi shows patients their own retinal photographs and explains findings directly — education that drives compliance with treatment and lifestyle changes.

🔦

Indirect Ophthalmoscopy & Volk Lens

Dilated examination with a binocular indirect ophthalmoscope and high-power Volk lens allows examination of the far peripheral retina — the zone where retinal breaks, tears, and lattice degeneration most commonly occur in myopes. This full peripheral examination is done on every patient at risk.

I've been diabetic for 5 years but my vision seems fine — do I need a retina check?

Yes — absolutely. Diabetic retinopathy has no symptoms in its early stages. The vision you experience has no reliable correlation with what is happening at the retinal level. Clinical studies show that ~20% of diabetics have retinopathy at the time of diagnosis. An annual retina exam is not optional — it is an essential component of diabetes management.

What does an anti-VEGF injection involve?

The eye is numbed with anaesthetic drops. A very fine needle introduces the anti-VEGF drug (bevacizumab/ranibizumab/aflibercept) into the vitreous cavity through the white of the eye. The procedure takes 5 minutes. Most patients feel mild pressure, not pain. Mild redness and increased floaters for 1–2 days are normal. The treatment reduces retinal fluid and protects or improves vision in diabetic macular oedema and wet AMD.

Can retinal detachment be prevented?

Yes — in many cases. Retinal breaks and tears, which are the precursors of detachment in most myopic patients, can be detected at screening and sealed with prophylactic laser photocoagulation before they progress. This is why high myopes need annual retina examinations even if they have no symptoms.

How long does a dilated retina examination take?

The dilation drops take 20–30 minutes to work. The examination itself takes 10–15 minutes. Total time including pre-tests: 45–60 minutes. Bring sunglasses — your near vision will be blurred for 3–4 hours after dilation. Do not drive yourself home after a dilated examination.
