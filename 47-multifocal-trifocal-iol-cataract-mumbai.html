Who Is Suitable for a Multifocal or Trifocal IOL?

A premium IOL is a significant investment and a permanent decision. Dr. Modi evaluates suitability carefully before recommending a multifocal or trifocal lens:

✅

Good Candidates

  • Active lifestyle — driving, reading, using a computer and phone
  • Healthy macula and optic nerve (confirmed by OCT)
  • Corneal astigmatism under 1.0 D (or corrected with toric multifocal)
  • Realistic expectations about night halos and glare (usually mild and reduce over time)
  • Not dependent on night driving at very high contrast
⚠️

Patients Better Suited to Monofocal

  • Significant macular degeneration or diabetic macular oedema
  • Glaucoma with moderate to advanced visual field loss
  • Corneal disease (keratoconus, pellucid marginal degeneration)
  • Professional night drivers (surgeons operating, pilots, truck drivers)
  • Very dry eye disease (affects optical quality of premium IOLs)

The Sentra Clinic Cataract Consultation — What to Expect

At your cataract consultation, we perform:

  1. A-scan biometry (IOLMaster): Precise measurement of your eye's axial length and corneal curvature to calculate the ideal IOL power. Accuracy here determines whether you are glasses-free after surgery.
  2. Corneal topography: Mapping your corneal curvature to detect astigmatism and any irregularities that would contraindicate premium IOLs.
  3. Macular OCT: Imaging the central retina to ensure no hidden disease that would reduce the benefit of a premium IOL.
  4. Lifestyle discussion: Dr. Modi spends time understanding your visual priorities — reading, driving, computer use, night activities — to recommend the IOL best matched to your life.

Frequently Asked Questions — Multifocal / Trifocal IOL

What is the difference between multifocal and trifocal IOL?

A multifocal IOL typically provides two clear focal points (distance and near). A trifocal IOL adds a third focal point — the intermediate distance (computer screen at ~60cm). Trifocal IOLs are generally better for today's screen-heavy lifestyles, as they provide continuous vision from near to far without the "jump" between focal points that older bifocal IOLs had.

Will I see halos around lights at night with a multifocal IOL?

Halos and glare are the most common side effects of multifocal IOLs. Most patients notice them in the first 3–6 months and then neuroadapt — the brain learns to ignore them. In properly selected patients (good macular health, low astigmatism), halos are mild and rarely affect daily life. Dr. Modi discusses this honestly at consultation so you make an informed decision.

Can a multifocal IOL be removed or replaced if I am unhappy?

IOL exchange (removing and replacing an implanted lens) is technically possible but is considered a complex surgery with risks. The best strategy is meticulous pre-operative assessment to select the right lens — which is exactly what the Sentra consultation protocol does. Unhappy patients after premium IOL surgery are rare at Sentra because of the thorough pre-op evaluation.
