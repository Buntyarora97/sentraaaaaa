The One Rule: Hours Matter

The retina and optic nerve are brain tissue — and like brain tissue during a stroke, they have a treatment window. Understanding this changes how you respond:

  • Retinal artery blockage ("eye stroke"): measured in hours. Like a heart attack, the cells of the retina die when their blood supply is cut. Treatment within the first 1–4 hours is meaningfully different from treatment 12 hours later.
  • Retinal detachment: loses cells daily. A detachment involving only the peripheral retina is very different from one that reaches the macula — and that difference is determined by how fast the patient arrives.
  • Optic nerve inflammation (optic neuritis): responds best to treatment started early.

The cruel twist is that most of these are painless — and painless is precisely what makes people wait. Reframe this now: sudden + one eye + vision change = same-day examination, pain or no pain.

6 Possible Causes — Serious to Simple

These six causes cover the overwhelming majority of sudden one-eye vision changes. Knowing them helps you understand why the doctor will not reassure you over the phone — and why coming in is the only way to sort urgent from benign:

⛵

1. Retinal Detachment

A curtain or shadow spreading across vision, typically preceded by a sudden shower of new floaters and flashes of light. Surgical emergency — each hour the detachment spreads increases the area of retina damaged. More about retinal detachment →

🧠

2. Retinal Vein/Artery Occlusion ("Eye Stroke")

Sudden painless dimming or blur in one eye, often in BP, diabetes or heart disease patients. Artery blockages are among ophthalmology's truest emergencies — time to treatment directly determines outcome.

🩸

3. Vitreous Haemorrhage

Bleeding inside the vitreous (gel) of the eye — sudden floaters-to-blackout spectrum. Common in advanced diabetics, or after a retinal tear. Requires urgent examination to find the underlying cause. Diabetic eye care →

🔴

4. Optic Neuritis

Blur combined with dull ache on eye movement and washed-out colours, often in younger patients. Needs urgent neuro-ophthalmic workup — it can be an early warning of systemic neurological conditions.

🌀

5. Macular Problems

Sudden central distortion — straight tiles, door-frames or text appears bent or wavy (wet macular degeneration). More common over 55. Time-sensitive — prompt anti-VEGF treatment slows progression significantly.

✨

6. Benign Mimics (But Still Need Diagnosis)

Migraine aura (shimmering zigzags, BOTH eyes, resolving in ~20–30 min), sugar-swing blur in diabetics, severe dry-eye film fluctuation. Diagnoses of relief — but diagnoses a doctor must make, not Google.

What We Check Immediately

A same-day emergency evaluation at Sentra Clinic includes:

🔦

Pupil Response Test

A torch-and-swing test that screens the optic nerve in under 60 seconds. A relative afferent pupillary defect (RAPD) is often the first sign of serious optic nerve or retinal pathology — detectable before any imaging.

👁️

Dilated Retinal Examination

Full examination of the retina, optic nerve, and vitreous with indirect ophthalmoscopy. A retinal detachment, vitreous haemorrhage, or occlusion can be directly visualised — no imaging needed for diagnosis.

📊

OCT Imaging

High-resolution imaging of the retinal layers confirms subtle macular pathology, quantifies swelling, and documents the baseline for treatment tracking. Results are available immediately in the clinic.

🩺

Pressure & Full Assessment

Eye pressure, visual acuity measurement, and history of BP, diabetes, cardiac history — all checked at the same sitting, since vascular eye emergencies often need same-week cardiovascular review.

Two outcomes are possible — and both reward the patient who came today: a benign cause confirmed with relief, or a serious cause caught inside its treatment window.

🚨 Why "Wait and Watch" Costs Vision

The most common phrase heard from patients arriving late with serious eye emergencies is: "I thought it would get better on its own." Retinal artery occlusions, detachments and acute macular haemorrhages do not get better on their own. They progress. The patients who arrive within the first few hours of onset have meaningfully different prognosis from those who wait three days to see if the blur resolves. Frequently Asked Questions

Can sudden blurry vision fix itself?

Some benign causes (migraine aura, sugar swings) resolve — but the dangerous causes don't, and early treatment defines the outcome. The same-day exam is how you find out which one you have. Waiting to see if it resolves is only safe if you already know the cause — and you can only know the cause after examination.

What is an eye stroke?

Blockage of the retina's blood supply — sudden, painless vision loss in one eye, commonest in BP, diabetes and heart patients. It is treated as a true emergency: treatment within the first hours may recover some vision that would otherwise be permanently lost. It also triggers a cardiovascular checkup, since eye strokes warn of body strokes.

Vision came back after a few minutes — do I still need a checkup?

Yes, urgently — this week. Transient vision loss (amaurosis fugax) can be a mini eye-stroke: a temporary blockage that cleared, warning of a larger one coming. It demands same-week cardiovascular and retinal evaluation. "It came back" is not relief; it is a warning you should not ignore.

Sudden floaters — is it an emergency?

A sudden shower of NEW floaters (not the old ones you have had for years) combined with flashes of light is a retinal tear warning until proven otherwise — come the same day. A single new floater without flashes can be a posterior vitreous detachment (common, usually benign) — still warrants same-week review. Do not manage new floaters with "wait and watch" without a dilated examination first.
